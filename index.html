Inside the model Definitions, assumptions & limits

Spatial distance. Euclidean distance between two occupied points: √((x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²), in metres. People sharing a point have zero spatial separation.

Social distance. The fewest friendship edges between two people. Same person = 0; direct friends = 1; disconnected = ∞. This is a metric within a connected group and an extended metric across disconnected groups. Locations inherit their occupants’ relationships; empty locations have no social distance.

Walking distance. A shortest route on a 2 m grid, simplified with collision-checked shortcuts around the pond and buildings. Approximate, not the exact continuous shortest path. Visitors are points; landscaping is schematic and passable. Motion follows these routes at 1.4 m/s, accelerated by the speed control.

Friendship in motion. The selected pair has priority: direct friends approach along a shared route, then walk to random destinations together with a small following gap. Selected nonfriends wander independently, even if they have other friends. Each remaining visitor chooses one available direct friend or wanders alone. Selecting the same person twice gives that visitor a solo walk. Routes avoid buildings and the pond; a detour can briefly increase straight-line separation. This is an illustrative rule, not a prediction of human behaviour.

Near and far. This experiment uses ≤40 m for spatially near and >40 m for far; ≤1 link for socially close, 2 for intermediate, and ≥3 for distant. These thresholds are illustrative, not universal. All people, friendships, and places are fictional.
